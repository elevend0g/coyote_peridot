# ..\Coy_Per\Encounter\Stage 0\Hull_Breach

_Coyote Peridot — Custodian Reference_

_Resolves before Level 1, before Power Tier progression begins. This is the Station's first observation of the group._

---

## Setup

The party's ship is clamped to one of the twelve facets. There is no door, no airlock, no lock to pick. Only a vast, seamless, faintly glowing surface that has to be convinced to open. This is a **3-roll skills challenge**, resolved as a group effort: three different player characters each make one independent Chaos Roll, in whatever order the table finds natural. The party should narrate who steps up for each attempt and why. This is a light, collaborative scene, not an interrogation of individual character sheets.

All three PCs are at **P1, Level 0**.

## The Three Rolls

Each roll uses a different Skill Slot. These are defined in the party's character sheets from the Coyote Peridot Skills document (Core Tier, 7 Skills):

| Stage | Domain | What's Happening |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | **Perception** | Reading the facet for any response. Texture changes, faint light shifts, anything that reacts to contact. |
| 2 | **Adaptation** | Adjusting technique based on what Stage 1 revealed. The surface doesn't respond to force; it responds to changed approach. |
| 3 | **Composure** | Holding steady through the uncertainty. Patience under a slowly mounting sense that something vast is deciding whether to notice you. |

These resolve in 1-2-3 order at the table, but narrating them in this sequence (look → adjust → hold) gives the scene a natural shape: notice something, try something, wait it out.

## Resolution Procedure

For each of the three rolls, run a standard Chaos Roll per the Base10 procedure:

1. The Custodian rolls 1d100 in secret (or openly, table's preference) against the acting character's Base Skill for that slot.
2. Roll ≤ Base Skill → automatic success.
3. Roll > Base Skill → the character rolls 1d100 + their Chaos Bonus for that slot, contesting the Custodian's result.

**Remember:** the Custodian's roll is made fresh, at the table, at the moment of each check. It is not predetermined. Don't pre-roll or look ahead; let the scene breathe.

Tally successes and failures across all three rolls.

## Outcome Table

| Fails | Result |
| :---- | :---- |
| 0–1 | **Clean breach.** The facet opens without further incident. Automatta encountered later remain in their default unreactive state — Encounter 1's maintenance units behave exactly as described in the Frame document (oblivious, route-bound, no aggression). |
| 2–3 | **Disruptive breach.** The party still gets in, the facet opens regardless, but the entry was forceful or fumbled enough that it registers as a disturbance. This flags Automatta encountered at Encounter 2 as aggressive instead of their default passive state. |

There is no failure state that locks the party out. The Station always lets them in. What changes is only how the Automatta react afterward. Entry is guaranteed; the only thing being tested is *how* they got in, which the Station quietly logs.

## Narration Notes for the Custodian

- This is the Station's **first data point** on the group, per the Frame document's design philosophy. It is "noticing how the group responds when their tools get them partway and no further." Treat each roll's success or failure as something the Station is registering, not just a mechanical gate.
- A group that **forces** the facet (treats failed rolls as "push harder") should feel that read back to them later, subtly, not punitively. A group that **adapts and waits** should feel rewarded in tone even on a failed roll: failure here should produce a strange, alien response, not a slammed door. The surface might dim, pulse oddly, or simply go still and unresponsive for a beat before the next attempt.
- Keep this **short** — this is the cold open, not the centerpiece. Three rolls, a few lines of description each, and through the door. The campaign proper starts on Level 1.
- Don't reveal the Stage 1 consequence (Automatta aggression) to the players. It's a flag for the Custodian, not information their characters have. Per the World/Player Guide documents, this is "another topic" learned through play, not exposition here.

## Stage 9 Ramifications

The results of this test are noted. A Clean Breach is marked as a +1 and a Disruptive Breach is marked as a -1 on the Core Tracking Table.
