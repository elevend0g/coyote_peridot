_A Base10 Content Module Supplement_

_Frame 2 Reality — Continuum11 Onboarding Module_

_An elevendog media llc publication_

---

*Two things live in this document. The first is the starting armory — what a character walks onto the hull with, before the facet ever opens. The second is a universal, tier-based drop table for anything that goes down fighting: rival crews, corrupted Automatta, security units. Room Loot (see Coyote Peridot Room Loot Tables) is what's sitting in the room regardless of what happens there. This is what's still in the mob's hands when it stops moving.*

*Armor is not addressed here. Per the Base10 System Guide, armor occupies the Chest carry slot and is issued at character creation according to Profession — that assignment belongs in the Coyote Peridot Professions document. What follows covers Weapon Slots (Primary, Secondary, Backup) and Carry Slots (Right Pocket, Left Pocket, Back) only.*

---

## How to Use — Starting Equipment

At character creation, after Profession and Faction are chosen, the player selects:

- **One Primary weapon** from the Primary catalog
- **One Secondary weapon** from the Secondary catalog
- **One Backup weapon** from the Backup catalog
- **One Skill Gear item**, filling one Pocket slot
- **One Narrative Gear item**, filling the other Pocket slot

The Back slot holds a standard-issue field pack. It has no mechanical stats of its own — per the System Guide, backpacks are pure carry expansion — but the Custodian may allow a second Narrative Gear pick into it at session zero if the table wants a slightly more generous loadout. That's a table-by-table call, not a rule.

All starting weapons are **P1** — one mod slot, empty, ready for whatever the party finds on Level 1. None are hardwired. What a character starts with is a foundation, not a finished build.

### Weapon Range Profiles (Baseline, P1)

The System Guide leaves specific range numbers to the Content Module. These are the Coyote Peridot baselines for starting gear. Point Blank and Long Range are both measured in hexes from the *Movement* section's range categories.

| Weapon Archetype | Point Blank Max | Long Range Max |
| :--- | :-: | :-: |
| Melee (Hand-to-Hand only) | — | — |
| Close-Range Projectile — Primary | 3 | 6 |
| Close-Range Projectile — Secondary | 2 | 4 |
| Close-Range Projectile — Hold-Out | 1 | 2 |
| Medium-Range Projectile — Primary | 6 | 12 |
| Medium-Range Projectile — Secondary | 4 | 8 |

Most Backup weapons are Hand-to-Hand by design — they're what's left when the fight is already on top of you. The Hold-Out Pistol is the exception: a last-ditch firearm, not a blade, for a character who wants a Backup option with reach.

## Skill Gear

_A small tool, kit, or rig built around one of the seven Core skills. Occupies one Pocket slot. Each grants **+1 Reroll (Chaos Roll, named Skill only)** — the same Skill Item mechanic used throughout the Room Loot Tables (a Content Module extension of standard Reroll rules), at P1 value. Two flavor options are offered per skill, sharing the identical effect — pick whichever fits the character, same pattern the Room Loot Tables use for paired items._

| Skill | Item | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perception** | Field Loupe **or** Threadcount Lens | A fold-out optic with a half-dozen filter settings, none of them labeled in a language anyone recognizes anymore — **or** a jeweler's lens for reading fine detail, wear patterns, and things that don't quite match. |
| **Adaptation** | Multi-Spanner **or** Contortionist's Kit | A collapsible toolkit built to reconfigure itself around whatever it's clamped onto — **or** a set of joint braces and grip aids for fitting a human body through spaces that weren't built for one. |
| **Composure** | Counting Chip **or** Breath-Timer Bead | A worn metal disc, etched with a repeating pattern meant to be traced with a thumb — **or** a small bead worn at the collar that pulses on a slow, steady interval, there to be breathed along with. |
| **Stealth** | Muffle Wrap **or** Silent-Step Insoles | A roll of dense, sound-dead cloth for binding boots, straps, or anything else that rattles — **or** a pair of insoles that swallow footfall on hard Station decking. |
| **Combat** | Honing Kit **or** Zero-Kit | A compact maintenance rig for keeping an edge sharp or a barrel true — **or** a sighting tool for zeroing a new weapon fast, useful the moment a mob drop replaces something familiar. |
| **Medicine** | Trauma Kit **or** Suture Pen | A flat case of sealants, clamps, and stabilizers, sized to fit in a pocket instead of a pack — **or** a self-dosing pen that closes a wound clean enough to worry about later. |
| **Influence** | Faction Chit **or** Introduction Chip | A stamped credential chip from the character's sponsoring faction — half ID, half calling card — **or** a recorded reference from someone whose name still opens doors, faction notwithstanding. |

## Narrative Gear

_No fixed mechanical effect. These exist to be used well by a player who's paying attention — the Custodian adjudicates outcomes case by case, the way the System Guide's own philosophy expects for skill-forward, non-combat problem solving. Occupies one Pocket slot (or the Back slot, at the Custodian's discretion — see How to Use above)._

| Item | Description |

## Mob Drop Tables

_A universal, tier-based table — separate from Room Loot. Roll once when a named hostile (a rival operative, a corrupted Automatta, a security unit) goes down in Combat. Use judgment on scale: roll once per notable kill, not once per faceless mook in a crowd scene, and once for the culminating unit of a Wave rather than every spawn along the way. Loot tier always matches the encounter's Power Tier — Level 1 mobs drop P1, Level 5 mobs drop P5, regardless of the party's own Power Tier (standard Kit and mod-slot access rules apply)._

_Rows 1–9 vary in category so a single table covers weapons, armor, and mods of every type in one roll — including, now, a rare chance at a full Primary weapon off a mob that was carrying one. Row 10 is the joke entry, trimmed down to a 10% weight (half the Room Loot Tables' rate — mob drops lean more mechanical, less incidental). Flavor alternates between "stripped from a fallen operative" and "salvaged from a broken unit" — pick whichever fits how the mob went down._
