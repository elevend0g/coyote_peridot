## Overview

A **Profession** is Coyote Peridot's implementation of the Base10 Class system. Every character belongs to one Profession, chosen at character creation. That choice is permanent. A Profession is not a role that can be reassigned; it is a dimension of identity, as fixed as the Attributes that define the character's nature.

Four Professions are available in Coyote Peridot. They are not faction-locked. A Corporate Shadow and an Institutional Shadow are both possible. The Profession is a skill set and a way of operating, not an ideology.

Each Profession consists of the following:

- **Five Class Features** - one unlocked at the .0 stage of each Power Tier (levels 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8)
- **Starting Bonuses** - pre-allocated Advancement Points and Attribute increases awarded at character creation, in addition to the standard 3 starting AP
- **Associated Kit** - a signature item the Profession is mechanically built around

*⚠️ [Base10] - The Class framework governing Profession mechanics - including Feature types, Starting Bonus rules, Tier Bonus caps, and the distinction between modifier types and Passive Effects - is defined in the Base10 System Guide. This document defines the Professions themselves within that framework.*

*⚠️ [Draft] - Class Feature definitions in this document represent design intent. Precise trigger language, Passive Effect resolution rules, and associated Kit statistics require mechanical review before final publication.*

## Playing Without a Profession

Playing without a Profession is always valid. A character defined entirely by their Attributes, Skills, and Kit is a complete and functional configuration. No mechanical gap is created by the absence of a Profession.

In Coyote Peridot, a character without a Profession is not a character without a purpose. They are the character the system has no category for - someone whose history is too varied, too interrupted, or too deliberate to fit a single professional frame. The Station does not care what your credentials are.

An unclassed character receives no Starting Bonuses and no Class Features. In exchange, they carry no professional identity that the Interface can read against them. Whether that is an advantage is something the campaign will determine.

*⚠️ [Custodian] - An unclassed character is not a lesser option. They are a different one. The Interface has no established read on them - no professional frame through which to interpret their choices. What that means at the Core is left to the Custodian's judgment based on the full arc of that character's behavior across all five levels.*

## Shadow

*Every shadow holds information. The question is whether you can read it.*

The Shadow operates in the spaces between declared intentions - reading people, moving through environments without friction, and acting on information others don't know they've given away. In a Station full of factions with competing agendas, the Shadow is the character who knows what everyone actually wants before the meeting starts.

The Shadow's power is informational. Their mechanical arc is about expanding access - more situations where their read on people and environments translates into advantage. By P5, the Shadow is operating in multiple registers simultaneously: social, physical, and informational.

**Role:** Navigating human politics, faction tension, and social encounter.

**Archetype:** Rogue

---

### Associated Kit - Neural Lace

The Neural Lace is a subdermal sensory augmentation that sharpens social and environmental reads. Passive enhancement, not a weapon. Micro-expression analysis, pheromone detection, ambient signal interception; the Lace feeds data into the Shadow's awareness below the threshold of conscious processing. They notice things they cannot explain noticing.

Higher-grade Laces expand the Lace's passive coverage and reduce the latency between signal and read. At peak grade, the Shadow is processing more information than they could consciously track - and acting on it correctly anyway.

---

### Starting Bonuses

| Bonus Type | Target | Amount | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Skill Starting Bonus | Influence | +1 AP | +10% to Influence base percentage |
| Skill Starting Bonus | Stealth | +1 AP | +10% to Stealth base percentage |
| Attribute Starting Bonus | Versatility | +1 | Versatility Attribute score increases by 1 at character creation |

These bonuses are awarded at character creation in addition to the character's normal 3 starting Advancement Points. Skill bonuses are pre-allocated and cannot be reallocated. The Versatility bonus is applied before play begins and is subject to the standard Attribute maximum of 9.

---

### Class Features

**P1 - Surface Read**
The Lace's micro-expression and pheromone analysis gives the Shadow an edge in any social encounter. When attempting an Influence Skill roll, the Shadow gains a Reroll (Chaos Roll).

**P2 - Ghost**
The Shadow has learned to move through spaces and systems without leaving a trace. When attempting a Stealth Skill roll, the Shadow gains a Reroll (Chaos Roll).

**P3 - Tell**
Under pressure, the Shadow identifies and acts on a critical vulnerability in an opponent - a hesitation, a misread, a moment of exposure. Once per Exchange, when the Shadow commits Attack dice, they may apply a Roll Bonus to that attack pool.

**P4 - Parallel Read**
The Shadow's upgraded Lace allows simultaneous management of social and physical awareness. Passive Effect: the Shadow may use Influence or Stealth during an Exchange without forfeiting their Defense Pool. Their Attack Pool is still forfeited. They remain a valid target and must respond to incoming attacks with no Attack dice available to counter.

**P5 - Full Spectrum**
At peak capability, the Shadow operates across social, physical, and informational registers simultaneously. Passive Effect: defined at the Custodian level based on campaign context and the Shadow's established capabilities. Specific mechanical expression is Custodian-adjudicated within module-defined parameters.

## Soldier

*The Station doesn't care about your feelings. Neither does the person shooting at you.*

The Soldier is trained for direct confrontation and has the discipline to function when everything else has gone wrong. Not a blunt instrument - a calibrated one. In a Station where not every threat is human and not every danger can be predicted, the Soldier is the character the group relies on when the situation stops being manageable.

The Soldier's power is reliability. Their mechanical arc is about sustaining performance under conditions that degrade everyone else - more dice, better dice, and the capacity to keep functioning when the situation is actively working against it. By P5, the Soldier is the most consistently dangerous combatant in any Exchange.

**Role:** Combat, direct threat response, group protection.

**Archetype:** Fighter

---

### Associated Kit - Combat Frame

The Combat Frame is a tactical exosuit layer - not full armor, not a weapon. A structural enhancement that extends the Soldier's physical capability and keeps them in the fight longer. Impact absorption, joint stabilization, reactive positioning assist. The Frame is the reason the Soldier can absorb what would stop anyone else and keep moving.

Higher-grade Frames extend the Frame's mechanical benefits and expand its reactive capacity. At peak grade, the Frame and the Soldier have become a single system; each responding to the other before conscious decision-making catches up.

## Tech

*You don't have to understand something to use it. But it helps.*

The Tech interfaces with systems - human and alien alike. They are the character most likely to make progress on what the Station's instruments are actually measuring, what the Commercial level's exchange interfaces were designed to do, and what the Core's control systems require. They are also the character most likely to break something by learning from it.

The Tech's power is access. Their mechanical arc is about expanding what counts as a system - starting with the familiar, ending with the entirely alien. By P5, the Tech is capable of engaging simultaneously with multiple systems, including potentially the Station's own infrastructure.

**Role:** Interfacing with Station technology, system analysis, problem-solving.

**Archetype:** Wizard

---

### Associated Kit - Interface Rig

The Interface Rig is a suite of hardware and software for engaging with both human and alien systems. Physical connection points, signal analyzers, adaptive translation layers, and a processing core that learns from every interface attempt - including the failed ones. The Rig is not a weapon. It is a vocabulary, expanding with use.

Higher-grade Rigs extend simultaneous connection capacity and reduce the cost of failed attempts. At peak grade, the Rig has accumulated enough interface history that it approaches alien systems with something like prior knowledge - pattern recognition built from every system the Tech has ever touched.

## Medic

*Everyone is fine until they aren't.*

The Medic keeps the group functional. In a Station where the hazards are poorly understood and help is far away, this is not a secondary role. The Medic is also the character with the most complete picture of what the Station is doing to the people inside it - physiologically, psychologically, and otherwise. In an advanced setting, the Medic carries more weight than a combat medic. They are the character tracking what prolonged exposure to Coyote Peridot actually costs.

The Medic's power is sustainability and understanding. Their mechanical arc is about expanding care - from immediate treatment to biological assessment to something that begins to look like ecological understanding of the Station itself. By P5, the Medic's read on the Station's biology extends beyond human physiology into whatever the Station is producing.

**Role:** Sustaining the group, managing consequences, medical and biological assessment.

**Archetype:** Support

---

### Associated Kit - Medical Rig

The Medical Rig is a compact diagnostic and treatment suite. Bioscanners, pharmaceutical synthesizers, tissue repair systems, and an environmental analysis layer that tracks atmospheric composition, biological contaminants, and physiological stress indicators. The Rig is always running - always taking readings, always comparing current data against baseline.

Higher-grade Rigs expand diagnostic range and treatment capacity. At peak grade, the Medical Rig is capable of assessing alien biology, not just human - building frameworks for organisms it has never encountered from first principles, the same way a good Medic does.

### Class Features

**P1 - Triage**
Fast assessment, faster response. The Rig is already running diagnostics before the Medic consciously decides to act. When attempting a Medicine Skill roll, the Medic gains a Reroll (Chaos Roll).

**P2 - Bedside Manner**
The Medic's reads on people are clinical as much as social - they understand what a body under stress does to the person inside it. When attempting an Influence Skill roll, the Medic gains a Reroll (Chaos Roll).

**P3 - Stabilize**
The Medic keeps the group in the fight by managing the consequences of the fight in real time. Passive Effect: once per Exchange, the Medic may prevent one teammate from advancing a Damage Tier when they would otherwise do so. The Medic is not attacking - they are managing what the Exchange is costing the group.

**P4 - Field Assessment**
The Rig handles active diagnostic work independently, allowing the Medic to maintain situational awareness while it runs. **P5 - Full Spectrum Care**

Passive Effect: defined at the Custodian level based on campaign context and the Medic's established capabilities.
