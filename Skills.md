## Overview

Coyote Peridot uses the **Core Skill Tier** - seven Skills drawn from the Base10 Skill Slot matrix. Every Attribute appears in exactly two Skill Slots. This configuration is designed for introductory play: broad enough to support four distinct Professions, focused enough that new players are never choosing between more options than the situation demands.

The Standoff Skill - used at the opening of every combat Exchange to determine Chain Order and assign the Edge - is **Perception** (Fo–In).

*⚠️ [Base10] - Skill mechanics, including Chaos Rolls, Skill Bonuses, Attribute Bonuses, and the Standoff procedure, are defined in the Base10 System Guide. This document defines the Skills themselves - their names, descriptions, and contextual applications within Coyote Peridot.*

## Skill Descriptions

### Medicine - Cu–Em (Curiosity + Empathy)

Medicine is not limited to field treatment. In a Station whose systems have been running for millennia, whose air composition shifts between levels, and whose hydroponic output has never been independently verified, the Medic is the character responsible for understanding what this environment is doing to the people inside it - and whether that is changing.

Medicine covers emergency treatment, pharmaceutical assessment, biological analysis, and the ongoing evaluation of human physiology under non-standard conditions. A character investing in Medicine is asking a question the Station has not yet answered: what does prolonged exposure here actually cost?

*Curiosity drives the investigation. Empathy calibrates the response - reading a patient, understanding a symptom, knowing the difference between what someone says and what their body is doing.*

#### *Example: Medicine (Coyote Peridot)*

The group has been on Level 3 for six hours. One character is reporting disorientation and an unusual metallic taste. The Custodian calls for a Medicine roll. Success tells the Medic that the symptoms match a pattern they have been tracking since Level 2 - something in the Station's atmospheric output is accumulating in human tissue. Failure means the Medic has data but no framework yet. Either way, the question has changed.

### Adaptation - Cu–Fo (Curiosity + Focus)

Adaptation is the capacity to encounter something genuinely unfamiliar and reorganize around it rather than defaulting to what you already know. It is not expertise. It is reconfiguration.

A character with high Adaptation does not necessarily know more than anyone else in the room. They get stuck less. Faced with an alien interface on Level 3, an Adaptation roll is not about prior knowledge of alien systems; it is about the cognitive flexibility to start building a framework from nothing, under pressure, and act on incomplete conclusions. Faced with a human problem that has stopped following recognizable rules, Adaptation is the skill that finds a new angle.

This is the skill the Interface is most interested in. The question it has been asking - *how do you move through a world you don't quite understand* - is Adaptation written in plain language.

*Curiosity opens the door. Focus builds something usable from what's on the other side.*

#### *Example: Adaptation (Coyote Peridot)*

The group reaches one of the exchange interfaces on Level 3. It responds to contact but in no pattern anyone can map to a known system. The Tech attempts an Adaptation roll. Success means they have identified a response loop - not what the interface does, but how to begin a conversation with it. Failure means the interface goes quiet. The Tech knows something went wrong. They do not know what.

---

### Influence - Em–In (Empathy + Intuition)

Influence covers the full spectrum of deliberate human interaction: negotiation, persuasion, deception, reading a room, and understanding what someone actually wants beneath what they are saying. In a Station where four factions are competing for the same outcome and none of them are fully disclosing their terms, the ability to navigate that landscape is not a secondary skill.

Influence does not require honesty. A Shadow using Influence to extract information from a Corporate representative and a Medic using Influence to calm a panicked crew member are making the same roll with very different intentions. The skill does not judge. The Interface might.

*Empathy reads the person. Intuition catches what they are not saying.*

#### *Example: Influence (Coyote Peridot)*

The group needs passage through a section of Level 2 that a Mercenary unit has locked down. The Shadow attempts an Influence roll to negotiate access without disclosing where the group is headed. Success opens the corridor - and potentially plants a false trail. Failure closes the conversation. The Mercenaries are now watching.

### Perception - Fo–In (Focus + Intuition)

Perception is the Standoff Skill - used at the opening of every combat Exchange to determine Chain Order and assign the Edge. It represents reading a situation for threats before they become immediate: tracking movement, clocking weapons, sensing who is about to break.

Outside of combat, Perception applies to any situation where the character is assessing an environment or a person for information they are not volunteering. A character scanning a new level of the Station for hazards before the group descends. A Shadow reading a faction representative who is not saying what they mean.

Perception does not tell a character what is happening. It tells them what is *about* to happen.

*Focus keeps attention deployed where it matters - no wasted observation, no missed signal. Intuition interprets what returns before the conscious mind catches up.*

#### *Example: Perception (Coyote Peridot)*

The group is entering Level 4 for the first time. Before they move into the space, the Custodian calls for Perception rolls. The character with the highest result notices that the instruments on the far wall are not measuring the room - they are tracking the group. This information does not change what happens next. It changes how the group thinks about what happens next.

---

### Stealth - Cn–Ve (Conditioning + Versatility)

Stealth covers movement through spaces without detection - physical concealment, noise discipline, behavioral camouflage in social environments, and the ongoing management of presence in a place where presence is a liability. In a Station where the security Automatta respond to ruckus and the factions are watching their flanks, the ability to not be noticed is not a niche capability.

Stealth is the Shadow's most distinctive skill, but its applications extend across all Professions. A Soldier who can move a group through contested space without triggering a security response is not playing a secondary role.

*Conditioning moves the body through the space with control - footfall, posture, breath. Versatility adapts the approach when the first one stops working.*

#### *Example: Stealth (Coyote Peridot)*

The group needs to move through a corridor where a Corporate patrol has established a checkpoint. The Shadow takes point and attempts a Stealth roll to find and exploit a gap in the patrol pattern. Success moves the group through without contact. Failure does not necessarily mean detection; it means the Shadow's read was wrong, and now the group is mid-corridor with no clean exit.

---

### Physique - Cn–Wi (Conditioning + Will)

Physique covers the full range of what a body trained and pushed to its limits can do - and take. It applies when a character needs to apply raw physical force to the world: forcing a sealed door, hauling a crewmate across an unstable floor, throwing something heavy with purpose. It applies equally when the world is applying force to the character: absorbing damage, enduring environmental stress, pushing through injury to complete something that still needs doing.

These are not two different skills wearing the same name. The body that can force the door is the same body that keeps moving after the door fights back. Physique is the measure of that body - what it has been built to do and what it refuses to stop doing.

In a Station that has been running for millennia without regard for human tolerances, the physical demands compound as the group descends. What Physique asks of a character on Level 2 is not what it asks on Level 5. The skill grows with the character. The Station's demands grow faster.

*Conditioning is the body that can. Will is the part that does anyway.*

#### *Example: Physique (Coyote Peridot)*

A pressure door on Level 3 has partially sealed; the mechanism is intact but the frame has warped, and the manual override requires force the mechanism was not designed to need. The Custodian calls for a Physique roll. Success forces the door and the group moves through. Failure does not mean the character stops trying. It means the door has not moved, something in the attempt has cost the character, and the corridor behind them is no longer as quiet as it was.

---

### Composure - Ve–Wi (Versatility + Will)

Composure is the skill of remaining functional when the situation is actively working against that outcome. It applies when the Station's environment becomes hostile in ways that are not physical - psychological pressure, sensory disorientation, the cumulative weight of prolonged exposure to spaces that were not designed for human beings. It also applies when a plan has failed completely and the group needs someone who can still act.

Composure is not the same as not being affected. It is continuing to operate in spite of being affected. A character who fails a Composure roll is not broken; they are human, in a place that is indifferent to that fact, making decisions under conditions that no amount of training fully prepares for.

Every Profession benefits from Composure. The Station is designed to need it.

*Versatility finds the functional response in an unfamiliar situation. Will sustains it.*

#### *Example: Composure (Coyote Peridot)*

The group has reached Level 5. The Industrial Core is operational in ways none of them anticipated, and the scale of what the Station is doing - has always been doing - is now visible. The Custodian calls for Composure rolls. Success means the character processes what they are seeing and remains capable of action. Failure does not incapacitate them. It means they act from a compromised position - and the Interface is watching how they handle that too.

## Profession Skill Relationships

No Skill is exclusive to a single Profession. The table below reflects primary and secondary relationships - where a Profession is most likely to invest and which Skills extend their capability into adjacent roles.

| Skill | Primary Profession | Secondary Access |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Medicine | Medic | Any character tracking the Station's biological effects |
| Adaptation | Tech | All - the Station eventually demands it from everyone |
| Influence | Shadow | Medic, any character navigating faction relationships |
| Perception | All | - Standoff Skill, universal relevance |
| Stealth | Shadow | Soldier (group movement), any character avoiding Automatta |
| Physique | Soldier | Any character who cannot afford to be helpless |
| Composure | All | - the Station is designed to need it |

*⚠️ [Custodian] - Advancement Point allocation is the player's decision, not the Profession's requirement. A Soldier who invests in Medicine and a Medic who invests in Physique are both valid configurations. The Profession defines the starting position, not the ceiling.*
